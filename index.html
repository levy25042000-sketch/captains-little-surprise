EXPERIMENT 01 / 06RECURSIVE STRUCTURES

The jump-drive sequence

🚀 ✦ 🌌
MISSION LOG 01 · DEEP SPACE

Captain J has launched from Earth. The jump-drive is counting through a recursive sequence, but its next coordinate is missing. Recover the seventh term to stabilise the first space jump.

TRANSMISSION FROM VY: “If you can hear this, Captain, I’m still out here. Please don’t take the scenic route.”

The navigation engine follows a recursive rule. Recover the next state.

1, 4, 15, 64, 325, 1956, ?

For n = 2, 3, 4, ... each term is generated by aₙ = n(aₙ₋₁ + 1), with a₁ = 1. What is the seventh term?

The transition from a₆ to a₇ uses n = 7.
Apply aₙ = n(aₙ₋₁ + 1), starting from a₁ = 1.

EXPERIMENT 02 / 06COORDINATION GAME

Two signals, two possible rendezvous

🛰️ 💫 🛸
MISSION LOG 02 · SIGNAL INTERCEPT

Captain J reaches a relay station and receives two possible signals. One is Vy’s distress beacon. The other is an alien decoy. He must choose whether to synchronise with the signal. Vy is trying to coordinate from the other side.

VY’S MESSAGE: “Captain, if you choose to meet me halfway, I’ll find a way to reach you. Don’t leave me guessing.”

Jonathan chooses a row; Vy chooses a column. They must independently navigate to one of two rendezvous beacons, Dock or Beacon. Payoffs are (Jonathan, Vy). They choose simultaneously, without knowing the other’s choice.

Vy · DockVy · Beacon
Jonathan · Dock3, 2 ♡0, 0
Jonathan · Beacon0, 02, 3

A mismatch leaves both stranded. A successful rendezvous gives each a different payoff depending on the beacon. Identify all pure-strategy Nash equilibria. Enter both outcomes in the order (Jonathan, Vy), separated by a semicolon.

A pure-strategy Nash equilibrium is a cell where neither player can improve their own payoff by changing their choice alone.
Check the two coordinated outcomes separately. Then test whether either player would benefit from switching beacons unilaterally.

EXPERIMENT 03 / 06BAYESIAN INFERENCE

The zombie signal

🐰 📡 🌍
MISSION LOG 03 · INCOMING SIGNAL FROM EARTH

Through the static, Captain J receives a distress signal from Earth. A little bunny appears on the screen and claims to be Vy’s assistant, saying it knows where she is being held. The transmission is badly corrupted. No one can confirm who is on the other side of the signal.

UNKNOWN TRANSMISSION: “Captain J? It's Bunny. I work with Vy. I know where they're keeping her. The infection scanners are still working — please, let me aboard.”

Bunny says they crossed a zombie-infected zone to reach the extraction point. Captain J cannot verify the story yet. The scanner tests for infection with 95% sensitivity and a 5% false-positive rate. Assume the zombie virus affects 1% of the remaining population, and these values are exact.

P(C)=0.01 · P(+|C)=0.95 · P(+|¬C)=0.05

A randomly selected person tests positive. What is P(C | +)? Give your answer as a percentage to one decimal place.

Use Bayes' theorem. The denominator is the total probability of a positive result.
Compute (0.95 × 0.01) / [(0.95 × 0.01) + (0.05 × 0.99)].

EXPERIMENT 04 / 06RSA · MODULAR ARITHMETIC

The alien cipher

👽 🔐 🛸
MISSION LOG 04 · ALIEN TERRITORY

Captain J is intercepted by an alien patrol near Station V2. The aliens have locked the navigation gate and encrypted the access code. Decrypt the transmission to restore the route to Vy.

ALIEN TRANSMISSION: “Human pilot. Your destination is sealed. Recover the plaintext, and the gate to Station V2 will open.”

A toy RSA system uses p = 5, q = 11 and public exponent e = 3. The ciphertext is c = 17. Use the standard RSA decryption procedure.

n = pq = 55   ·   φ(n) = 40

Find the plaintext m, where m ≡ cᵈ (mod n) and d is the modular inverse of e modulo φ(n). Enter the integer m.

First solve 3d ≡ 1 (mod 40) using the extended Euclidean algorithm.
d = 27. Now calculate 17²⁷ mod 55 using repeated squaring.

EXPERIMENT 05 / 06COURNOT DUOPOLY

The market-clearing code

🏭 ⚡ 🛸
MISSION LOG 05 · THE ALIEN ENERGY MARKET

The portal is powered by an alien energy market controlled by two rival suppliers. Both firms choose how much energy to generate at the same time. Captain J must predict their Cournot equilibrium to calculate the portal's activation code.

LAST MESSAGE FROM VY: “Almost there, Captain. Think carefully about what each supplier will produce when neither can improve by changing output alone.”

Two identical firms compete in quantities. Market inverse demand is P = 150 − Q, where Q = q₁ + q₂. Each firm has constant marginal cost MC = 30 and zero fixed cost. They choose quantities simultaneously.

πᵢ = (P − 30)qᵢ   ·   P = 150 − q₁ − q₂

Find the symmetric Cournot-Nash equilibrium. Calculate each firm's equilibrium output, then the total market output Q* and equilibrium price P*. The portal code is Q* + P*. Enter the resulting integer.

Assume both firms are profit-maximising and there are no capacity constraints.

Write firm 1's profit as π₁ = (150 − q₁ − q₂ − 30)q₁. Differentiate with respect to q₁ to obtain its best-response function.
In a symmetric equilibrium q₁ = q₂. Solve both best responses to get each firm's output. Then calculate Q* = q₁ + q₂, P* = 150 − Q*, and add Q* + P*.
